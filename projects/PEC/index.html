When Policy Entropy Constraint Fails: Preserving Diversity in Flow-based RLHF via Perceptual Entropy

Abstract

RLHF is widely used to align flow-matching text-to-image models with human preferences, but often leads to severe diversity collapse after fine-tuning. In RL, diversity is often assumed to correlate with policy entropy, motivating entropy regularization. However, we show this intuition breaks in flow models: policy entropy remains constant, even while perceptual diversity collapses. We explain this mismatch both theoretically and empirically: the constant entropy arises from the fixed, pre-defined noise schedule, while the diversity collapse is driven by the mode-seeking nature of policy gradients. As a result, policy entropy fails to prevent the model from converging to a narrow high-reward region in the perceptual space. To this end, we introduce perceptual entropy that captures diversity in a perceptual space and maintains the property of standard entropy. Building upon this insight, we propose two entropy-regularized strategies, Perceptual Entropy Constraint (Perc-EC) and Perceptual VAE Constraint (Perc-VC), to preserve perceptual diversity and improve generation quality. Experiments across two base models (FLUX.dev, SD3.5-M), neural and rule-based rewards, and three perceptual spaces (PickScore, DINO, CLIP) demonstrate consistent gains in the quality-diversity trade-off; Perc-EC achieves the best overall score of 0.755 (vs. Flow-GRPO's 0.347) with a diversity average of 0.941 (vs. Flow-GRPO's 0.010).

Motivation: The Entropy Paradox

The Paradox

In LLM RL, an entropy decrease directly signals diversity loss. But in flow models, we observe a striking paradox: policy entropy stays constant while perceptual diversity collapses. This means existing entropy-based regularization methods fundamentally cannot capture or prevent diversity collapse in flow-based RLHF.

Figure 1. Training-time metrics. (a) Policy entropy and variance in VAE space — both remain constant. (b) Variance in pixel and reward space — diversity really collapses despite constant policy entropy.

Why Does Policy Entropy Stay Constant?

We prove that the policy entropy in flow-based RL is independent of model parameters $\theta$. The key insight: in flow models, the reverse transition $p_\theta(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}\mid\mathbf{x}_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}; \mu_\theta, \sigma_t^2\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathbf{I})$ has variance fixed by the prescribed noise schedule $\sigma_t$, while only the mean $\mu_\theta$ is learned. Since entropy depends on the shape (variance), not the location (mean), it remains constant:

$\mathcal{H}(p_\theta) = \frac{d}{2} - C_t$

Why Does Diversity Still Collapse?

We trace the collapse to the mode-seeking nature of on-policy policy gradients. With $p_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$ and the advantage $\mathcal{A}$ fixed within an update, the policy gradient locally minimizes a reverse KL to an advantage-induced target $p_{\mathcal{A}} \propto p_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}\exp(\mathcal{A})$:

$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{J}(\theta) = \nabla_\theta \Big( -D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_\theta \,\|\, p_{\mathcal{A}}\big) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_\theta \,\|\, p_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}\big) \Big)$

Since $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ is sampled from the old policy, unexplored perceptual modes are excluded from optimization, and repeated on-policy updates concentrate probability mass on a single high-reward mode. This motivates an RLHF framework that encourages broad coverage of multiple high-reward regions in perceptual space, rather than convergence to a single peak.

Figure 2. Optimization behavior. GRPO shows mode-seeking behavior and converges to a single-peak distribution, while an ideal model should encourage coverage of multiple regions.

Figure 3. Training dynamics metrics. (a) Variance of reward and standard deviation of samples; (b) maximum and minimum advantage probabilities $p_{\mathcal{A}}$ during training. Both decline, consistent with mode-seeking contraction.

Method: Perceptual Entropy

Intuitively, diversity collapse can be seen as the feature variance reduction in human perception. Our analysis reveals a key factor: the standard policy entropy in flow models is unable to capture this collapse, undermining the effectiveness of existing entropy-based regularization for flow matching. This motivates a new entropy concept, perceptual entropy, which captures diversity collapse in perceptual space while remaining consistent with existing entropy-based regularization methods.

Insight (Representation Perspective)

Standard VAEs primarily extract low-level features, making VAE-space entropy $\mathcal{H}$ insensitive to semantic diversity. For example, slightly altering eye size in a portrait shifts VAE latents while leaving high-level semantics nearly unchanged. In contrast, perceptual entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{perc}}$ in a high-level representation space directly captures semantic variations and reliably detects diversity collapse.

Perceptual Entropy

Perceptual entropy preserves the structure of standard policy entropy but is defined in the perceptual space rather than the generative latent space. Let $\phi = \mathcal{D}_{\text{vae}} \circ \mathcal{E}_{p}$, where $\mathcal{E}_{p}$ is a perceptual encoder and $\mathcal{D}_{\text{vae}}$ is a VAE decoder. Given $k$ rollout noised samples, the perceptual probability and perceptual entropy are:

$$p_\theta^{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k\mid\mathbf{x}_t^k,c) := \mathcal{N}\big(\phi(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k);\,\phi(\mathbf{m}^k),\, \sigma_t^2\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathbf{I}\big), \qquad \mathcal{H}_{\text{perc}}(p_\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,k}\big[-\log p_\theta^{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k\mid\mathbf{x}_t^k,c)\big]$$

where $\mathbf{m}^k$ denotes the old-policy mean $\mu_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\mathbf{x}_t^k,t,c)$. For neural network-based rewards, we use the PickScore encoder as $\mathcal{E}_{p}$ to avoid additional computational cost; frozen DINO or CLIP encoders also work.

Remark (Connection to Conditional Feature-Space Variance)

Let $\mathbf{z}_{t-1}=\phi(\mathbf{x}_{t-1})$. Assume that, within a local reverse step, the perceptual encoder is approximately mean-preserving, i.e., $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{z}_{t-1}\mid \mathbf{x}_t]\approx \phi(\mathbf{m})$. Then perceptual entropy tracks the expected conditional feature-space variance: $$\mathcal{H}_{\text{perc}}(p_\theta) \approx \mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_t}\!\left[\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{z}_{t-1}\mid \mathbf{x}_t)}{2\sigma_t^2\,\mathrm{d}t}\right] - C_t.$$ Perceptual entropy thus captures local perceptual dispersion, paralleling the latent-space variance relation of constant policy entropy and aligning with entropy-regularization analyses in LLMs.

Empirical Entropy Mechanism

In analogy to the empirical finding in LLMs, we propose a similar empirical entropy mechanism for flow models, where $a$ and $b$ are empirical coefficients:

$\mathcal{R} = -a\exp(\mathcal{H}_{\text{perc}}) + b$

Figure 4. Relationship between Reward and Perceptual Entropy. Perceptual entropy provides an effective identifier of diversity, aligning with existing research for LLMs.

Perceptual Entropy Constraint (Perc-EC)

Since perceptual entropy is a reliable surrogate for conditional variance, Perc-EC maximizes it directly: $\max_\theta\, \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim p_\theta}[\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x},c)] + \lambda\,\mathcal{H}_{\text{perc}}(p_\theta)$. To avoid competing gradients against the primary reward, we use perceptual reward shaping: the constraint is folded into the reward so that perceptual and task rewards are jointly normalized within the same GRPO rollout group:

$\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\textit{Perc-EC}}^k = \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}_0^k, c) - \lambda\,\mathbb{E}_t\big[\log p_\theta^{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k\mid\mathbf{x}_t^k,c)\big]$

Perceptual VAE Constraint (Perc-VC)

Perc-VC instead anchors the collapsed perceptual distribution to the diversity-preserving generation-space prior by minimizing $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_\theta^{\mathrm{perc}}\Vert p_\theta^{\mathrm{vae}})$, again incorporated as a shaped reward:

$\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\textit{Perc-VC}}^k = \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}_0^k,c) - \lambda\,\mathbb{E}_t\big[\log p_\theta^{\mathrm{perc}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k\mid\mathbf{x}_t^k,c) - \log p_\theta^{\mathrm{vae}}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}^k\mid\mathbf{x}_t^k,c)\big]$

Both shaped rewards are optimized with the standard GRPO clipped surrogate.

Experiments

We build upon Flow-GRPO and evaluate FLUX.dev (PickScore reward, trained on Pick-a-Pic) and SD3.5-M (rule-based rewards). Quality is measured on the HPD test set with PickScore, Aesthetic v2.5, ImageReward, CLIP, HPSv2.1 and Unified Reward; diversity is measured on 100 images per prompt over 85 PartiPrompts (8,500 images per method) with feature-space $L_2$ diversity and Vendi Scores. The overall score averages min-max normalized quality and diversity metrics.

Comparison with Entropy-Based Regularization

Native per-step entropy $\mathcal{H}$ provides limited gains under flow matching: standard entropy regularization reaches an overall score of 0.245, comparable to Flow-GRPO w/ KL (0.242). Simply replacing $\mathcal{H}$ with $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{perc}}$ raises it to 0.427, and Perc-EC ($\lambda=0.10$, PickScore) achieves the best overall score of 0.755 with a diversity average of 0.941.

Table 1. Entropy Regularization Comparison. Gray-shaded rows denote our methods. P.S.=PickScore, I.R.=ImageReward, U.R.=Unified Reward, V.S.=Vendi Score.

Comparison with SoTA Models

Existing text-to-image models exhibit a sharp quality–diversity trade-off: SDXL achieves the highest baseline diversity (0.651) but low quality (0.205), whereas Qwen-Image attains high quality (0.711) with a diversity of 0.000. In contrast, Perc-EC ($\lambda=0.10$, PickScore) reaches the best overall score of 0.751 with a diversity average of 0.872.

Ablation: Perceptual Entropy & Computational Cost

Replacing the native per-step entropy $\mathcal{H}$ with perceptual entropy $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{perc}}$ inside Perc-EC improves every metric (e.g., Aes.v2.5 from 6.372 to 6.412). The perceptual encoder is much smaller than the flow model and is evaluated asynchronously from generation and optimization, so the iteration-time overhead is marginal and peak memory is nearly unchanged.

Generalization to Text-to-Video and DDPO

On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B (Flow-GRPO with VideoPickScore in UniRL), both Flow-GRPO and native entropy regularization erode within-group perceptual variance as reward rises, whereas Perc-EC preserves perceptual diversity alongside high reward. Under DDPO, native entropy regularization still loses variance at $\lambda=0.05$ and destabilizes training at $\lambda=0.10$; Perc-EC maintains perceptual variance alongside high reward, and Perc-VC enables a controllable quality–diversity trade-off.

User Study

In a blind, randomized, side-by-side study comparing Perc-EC with Flow-GRPO, 20 volunteers provided 100 judgments over 20 unique image-set pairs (five independent judgments per pair). Perc-EC is preferred in 64% of overall judgments and 82% of diversity judgments, while image quality is rated comparably (38% Perc-EC vs. 43% Flow-GRPO, 19% no preference).
