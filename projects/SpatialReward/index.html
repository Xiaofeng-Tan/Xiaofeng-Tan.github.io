Getting Space Right:
Agentic Rewards for Image Generation

SpatialCritic

Spatial text-to-image generation requires faithful spatial relations, yet RL post-training often assumes both reliable rewards and effectively organized rollouts.

Summary. We study these two assumptions and propose a curriculum-guided RL framework. SpatialCritic verifies spatial claims through a perception harness and specialized vision tools; a compositional capability graph then schedules rollouts from foundational skills to complex compositions. The 8B critic reaches 95.08% verification accuracy on SpaRW-Eval, and the full framework raises average generation scores from 78.4 to 83.9 on SD3.5-Medium and 83.2 to 86.5 on BAGEL.

Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) is promising for spatial text-to-image generation, but existing work largely focuses on policy optimization, assuming accurate rewards and effective online rollouts. We observe in-domain gains with out-of-domain degradation, exposing two limitations: unreliable spatial rewards in open-world settings and rollouts lacking basic-to-complex progression. We propose a curriculum-guided RL framework. First, we introduce SpatialCritic, an agentic reward model equipped with a spatial perception harness that combines reusable verification guidance with specialized vision tools. We then construct spatial preference datasets through targeted counterfactual perturbations and perform agent–harness alignment to improve tool selection and evidence integration efficiently. Second, curriculum learning organizes shared foundational capabilities and their compositional dependencies in a capability graph for mastery-guided rollouts, periodic review, and targeted remediation. On SpaRW-Eval, SpatialCritic achieves 95.08% verification accuracy, outperforming 320B GLM-5.3-Flash with a 40× smaller 8B backbone and raising average baseline scores from 78.4 to 83.9 on SD3.5-Medium and 83.2 to 86.5 on BAGEL.

Introduction

Enhancing spatial intelligence in text-to-image generation—producing spatial relationships that are faithful to the prompt—remains a critical challenge in post-training. RL has become a prevailing paradigm for aligning pretrained generators with task-specific objectives, but its effectiveness depends jointly on rollout organization, reward accuracy, and policy optimization. Existing work primarily advances the third factor, often assuming that the other two are already reliable.

Two research questions
Q1Are reward models sufficiently accurate and robust in open-world spatial scenarios?
Q2Can rollout organization support progressive learning from basic spatial capabilities to complex compositions?

This failure arises when benchmark suites and their rule-based scoring pipelines are repurposed as training prompts and online rewards. Such rewards generalize poorly to open-world scenes, particularly under occlusion, while reused evaluation prompts emphasize joint constraint satisfaction rather than the foundational capabilities that compose into complex spatial relations.

Learned reward models partially address this brittleness, but regression-based scorers treat vision foundation models as black-box feature extractors, and generative evaluators rely on ungrounded teacher generation. Neither paradigm explicitly resolves geometric quantities such as occlusion, orientation, or depth ordering. SpatialCritic instead grounds verification in tool-mediated evidence, and the curriculum addresses the complementary rollout-organization problem.

Contributions

  1. An agentic reward model. SpatialCritic combines a VLM policy with a spatial perception harness, decomposes prompts into atomic claims, and selectively invokes specialized vision tools under uncertainty.
  2. Verifiable preference learning. Counterfactual spatial preference pairs and RLVR align the agent’s tool selection and evidence integration, improving both judgment accuracy and efficiency.
  3. A capability-guided curriculum. A compositional capability graph organizes rollouts from foundational spatial skills to complex compositions, with mastery-based progression, review, and remediation.

SpatialCritic: Agentic Reward Modeling

Spatial Perception Harness

  1. 1

    Decompose

    Separate object, attribute, and relation claims. Identify what remains uncertain.

  2. 2

    Plan & execute

    Obtain targeted evidence using grounding, counting, segmentation, depth, or orientation tools.

  3. 3

    Integrate evidence

    Resolve the outstanding claims and produce an image-pair preference verdict.

The tools provide observations. The agent must still interpret them and make an evidence-consistent decision.

Agent–Harness Alignment

A correct observation can still lead to an incorrect verdict. In the candle example, both trajectories receive the count evidence: three candles in A and two in B. The unaligned agent nevertheless makes an inconsistent final choice. Agent–harness alignment improves how the policy uses the evidence, rather than merely adding more tools.

We use verifiable outcome rewards and a bounded token-budget penalty to align the agent’s tool-selection and evidence-integration policy. The harness and perceptual tools remain fixed. With the same 8B backbone, alignment improves SpaRW-Eval accuracy from 92.91% to 95.08%.

Verifiable Spatial Preference Dataset

We construct counterfactual prompt pairs intended to violate one spatial constraint while preserving the others. Text-level checks, image-level verification, and sampled human inspection filter the pairs, so that the final preference reflects the actual visual contrast.

Curriculum Learning for Spatial Generation

A reliable reward tells the generator how it did. The curriculum determines what it should practice next. A compositional capability graph represents shared prerequisites rather than imposing a single easy-to-hard ordering.

Example dependencies

Topology + Depth → Occlusion

  1. 1

    Advance

    Unlock dependent capabilities when their prerequisites meet the progression criterion.

  2. 2

    Review

    Reassess learned skills in individual and compositional tasks.

  3. 3

    Remediate

    Target the capabilities that fall below the mastery threshold.
